## ADA Nutrition Therapy for Adults With Diabetes or Prediabetes (2019) - paraphrased excerpts

Section: macronutrient distribution
Evidence suggests that there is no ideal percentage of calories from carbohydrate, protein, and fat for all people with diabetes; therefore macronutrient distribution should be based on individualized assessment of current eating patterns, preferences, and metabolic goals.

Section: carbohydrate quality
People with diabetes should consume at least the recommended amount of fiber and whole grains for the general public, with emphasis on minimally processed, nutrient-dense whole foods over refined grains and foods high in added sugars.

Section: dietary fiber
Daily fiber intake of at least 14 g per 1,000 kcal (approximately 25-35 g/day) is associated with improved glycemic control and lower cardiovascular risk in people with type 2 diabetes.

Section: sodium
Recommendation for sodium intake for people with diabetes is the same as for the general population: less than 2,300 mg/day. Further reductions may be appropriate for individuals with both diabetes and hypertension.

Section: sweetened beverages
People with diabetes and prediabetes should reduce or eliminate intake of sugar-sweetened beverages (including fruit juices) to control glycemia and reduce risk for cardiometabolic disease.
