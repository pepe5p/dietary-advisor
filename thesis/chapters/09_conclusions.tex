\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{cha:conclusions}

This thesis set out to measure how much two grounding and review mechanisms -- a deterministic nutrient totaller and a critique-and-refine loop -- contribute to the reliability of an LLM-based one-day meal planner, on top of a food knowledge base the planner is already required to query rather than guess from. The resulting system enforces that requirement structurally: its language model never has a field in which to write a nutrient value, so every number in a delivered plan is traceable to a real food database entry and an exact arithmetic computation (\Cref{sec:impl-portion-ref,sec:design-no-invented-foods}).

Measured against that grounded baseline, enabling the deterministic totaller cut energy and macronutrient error from \qty{13.46}{\percent} to \qty{3.02}{\percent} for about a third more runtime, improved every one of the five scenarios and reduced the run-to-run standard deviation by an order of magnitude, which supports H1~through the cheaper of the two mechanisms. The critique-and-refine loop added nothing measurable on top of it: \qty{3.25}{\percent} against \qty{3.02}{\percent} is within the standard error of both means, and it roughly tripled wall-clock time; H2~is unsupported, since no judge's preference or safety score responded to enabling it. H3~is supported in all three of its parts -- plan quality varied with the model, from \qty{1.87}{\percent} to \qty{13.39}{\percent} mean error and \num{0.808} to \num{0.730} soft score across the four compared; it varied with the reasoning-effort level, monotonically from \qty{8.66}{\percent} to \qty{2.64}{\percent} pooled across models and within each one individually; and raising the weakest model's effort recovered roughly three quarters of its accuracy deficit. The practical caveat is that this was still not the right trade: the strongest model at its \emph{lowest} effort beat the effort-boosted weak model on both dimensions for \qty{14}{\second} more per plan.

Two secondary findings are worth carrying forward. The two evaluation dimensions do not order the configurations the same way, so neither is a proxy for the other: the two configurations tied for lowest energy and macronutrient error differ sharply in how well the judges think their plans answer the request, and reasoning effort improves accuracy monotonically while leaving preference satisfaction flat past its middle setting. And the two judges, while agreeing on almost every ranking, disagree systematically on absolute scores and, less excusably for a question with an objectively checkable answer, on where allergen and diet-pattern compliance actually slipped, which suggests hard exclusions deserve a deterministic checker rather than a judged score.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusions-limitations}

The evaluation is bounded in scope in ways that should temper how broadly its conclusions are read. The scenario set is five profiles, chosen for the clarity of their associated dietary guidance and for the variety of demands they place on the planner rather than for coverage of the conditions a real deployment would encounter. Both judges are language models: pairing two vendors makes family-specific bias visible, and \Cref{sec:results-stability} shows it did not disturb the rankings, but a bias the two share would still be invisible, and no human expert reviewed a single plan. Reasoning-effort levels are vendor-defined labels rather than calibrated quantities, so effort comparisons hold within a model but only indicate a direction across models. Finally, the system produces a single, non-interactive plan per session; nothing here speaks to how these mechanisms would behave across a multi-turn, incrementally edited planning conversation.

\section{Further work}
\label{sec:conclusions-future-work}

Several directions follow directly from the limitations of \Cref{sec:conclusions-limitations} and from the paths not taken in \Cref{sec:impl-dead-ends}.

\textbf{Retrieval-augmented clinical guidance.} The dietary guardrails in every agent's prompt are stated inline rather than retrieved, on the reasoning given in \Cref{sec:design-agents} that they apply near-universally to a healthy adult. Guidance that varies substantially with a diagnosed condition does not fit that reasoning, and the chronic-condition scenarios are exactly where it would matter most. Retrieving condition-specific guidance from a curated clinical corpus, in the manner \textsc{DietQA} applies to recipe data (\Cref{sec:background-related-systems}), would add a third grounding mechanism to the two measured here -- and, being independently switchable like the others, one whose contribution the same ablation design could quantify.

\textbf{Probability-weighted judge scores.} G-Eval weights a judge's score by the scoring model's token probabilities; this work estimates that quantity by averaging repeatedly sampled verdicts instead, because the judge APIs used expose no token probabilities (\Cref{sec:methodology-qualitative})~\cite{liu2023geval}. A judge served with them would yield the weighted score directly, removing both the sampling cost of the repetition axis and the residual judge noise that \Cref{sec:results-stability} has to report around it.

\textbf{Pairwise comparison between configurations.} Every qualitative score here is absolute: a plan is read against a rubric without reference to any other plan. The judge literature's stronger signal is a forced choice between two plans for the same profile, which agrees with human preference on \qty{85}{\percent} of non-tied comparisons (\Cref{sec:background-evaluation})~\cite{zheng2023judging}. Adding it would sharpen precisely the comparisons this thesis found hardest to call -- the configurations that tied on accuracy but not on preference -- at the cost of a comparison count quadratic in the number of variants and of randomising presentation order against the documented position bias.

\textbf{Asymmetric critic model.} Whether a critic benefits from being a stronger model than the generator it reviews was left open in \Cref{sec:impl-critic-design}: the pipeline already supports a different model per role, but the grid holds critic and generator identical to keep the number of independent variables manageable. Since the critique-and-refine loop's null result (\Cref{sec:results-ablation}) was obtained under that constraint, it bounds only what a self-review by an equally capable model achieves, and says nothing about a genuinely stronger reviewer.

\textbf{Multi-turn interactive refinement.} Extending the system to accept a follow-up request against an already-generated plan, rather than only a fresh session each time, was set aside in \Cref{sec:impl-dead-ends} as orthogonal to this thesis's research question; it remains a natural and comparatively well-scoped extension, since the critique-and-refine loop's revise-and-recheck pattern (\Cref{sec:design-reflection}) already provides most of the machinery a human-in-the-loop version of the same pattern would need.

\textbf{Model-generated food descriptions for retrieval.} \Cref{sec:impl-dead-ends} also set aside having a language model author a richer description of each food catalogue entry specifically to improve the text embedded for dense retrieval (\Cref{sec:impl-hybrid-search}), instead of the fixed field-concatenation rule used throughout this work. Measuring whether such descriptions actually improve retrieval quality, and by how much relative to the added offline build cost, is left as an open question.

\textbf{Elicitation of missing profile data.} The system currently assumes a complete, already-known user profile; a natural extension is a short elicitation dialogue that asks a small number of targeted follow-up questions when a profile is incomplete in a way that would materially affect the computed nutrient targets, rather than requiring every field to be supplied up front.
