\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{cha:conclusions}

This thesis measured how much two mechanisms -- a deterministic nutrient totaller and a critique-and-refine loop -- contribute to the reliability of an LLM-based one-day meal planner built on a queried food knowledge base. The system enforces grounding structurally: the language model has no field in which to write a nutrient value, so every number in a delivered plan traces to a real food database entry and an exact computation (\Cref{sec:impl-portion-ref,sec:design-no-invented-foods}).

H1 is supported by the cheaper mechanism: the totaller cuts energy and macronutrient error sharply in every scenario and makes plans more predictable. H2 is unsupported: the critique-and-refine loop adds nothing measurable on top of the totaller on either evaluation dimension while costing roughly three times the wall-clock time. H3 is supported in all three parts -- plan quality varies with model and reasoning effort, and raising a weaker model's effort recovers a substantial part of the gap -- with the caveat that spending the same latency on a stronger model at low effort still dominates (\Cref{sec:results-ablation,sec:results-model-comparison,sec:results-overhead}).

Two secondary findings carry forward. The two evaluation dimensions do not order the configurations the same way, so neither is a proxy for the other: the configurations tied for lowest energy and macronutrient error differ sharply in judged fit to the request, and reasoning effort improves accuracy monotonically while leaving preference satisfaction flat past its middle setting. And the two judges, while agreeing on almost every ranking, disagree systematically on absolute scores and, on the objectively checkable question of where allergen and diet-pattern compliance slipped, which suggests hard exclusions deserve a deterministic checker, not a judged score.

Those verdicts should be read inside the bounds of \Cref{sec:results-threats}: a five-scenario grid, LLM judges without a human expert, vendor-defined effort labels, a single non-interactive session, and a snapshot of hosted models that will not stay still.

\section{Further work}
\label{sec:conclusions-future-work}

Several directions follow directly from the limitations of \Cref{sec:results-threats}.

\textbf{Retrieval-augmented clinical guidance.} The dietary guardrails in every agent's prompt are stated inline because they apply near-universally to a healthy adult (\Cref{sec:design-agents}); guidance that varies with a diagnosed condition does not fit that reasoning, and the chronic-condition scenarios are where it would matter most. Retrieving condition-specific guidance from a curated clinical corpus, as \textsc{DietQA} applies to recipe data (\Cref{sec:background-related-systems}), would add a third grounding mechanism whose contribution, being independently switchable, the same ablation design could quantify.

\textbf{Probability-weighted judge scores.} A judge that exposed token probabilities would yield G-Eval's weighted score directly (\Cref{sec:methodology-qualitative}), removing both the sampling cost of the repetition axis and the residual judge noise that \Cref{sec:results-stability} has to report around it.

\textbf{Pairwise comparison between configurations.} Every qualitative score here is absolute: a plan is read against a rubric without reference to any other plan. A forced choice between two plans for the same profile agrees with human preference on \qty{85}{\percent} of non-tied comparisons (\Cref{sec:background-evaluation})~\cite{zheng2023judging}, and would sharpen the comparisons this thesis found hardest to call -- the configurations that tied on accuracy but not on preference -- at the cost of a comparison count quadratic in the number of variants and of randomising presentation order against the documented position bias.

\textbf{Asymmetric critic model.} Whether a critic benefits from being a stronger model than the generator it reviews was left open in \Cref{sec:impl-critic-design}. Since the critique-and-refine loop's null result (\Cref{sec:results-ablation}) was obtained under an equal-capability critic, it bounds only what a self-review by an equally capable model achieves.

\textbf{Multi-turn interactive refinement.} Accepting a follow-up request against an already-generated plan was excluded from the single-session scope of \Cref{sec:vision-usage-scenario}; it is a well-scoped extension, since the critique-and-refine loop's revise-and-recheck pattern (\Cref{sec:design-reflection}) already provides most of the machinery a human-in-the-loop version would need.

\textbf{Model-generated food descriptions for retrieval.} The text embedded for dense retrieval is assembled by a fixed field-concatenation rule (\Cref{sec:impl-hybrid-search}). Having a language model instead author a richer description of each food catalogue entry could normalise inconsistent crowd-sourced data, at the cost of an offline LLM-dependent build step; whether it improves retrieval quality enough to justify that cost is an open question.

\textbf{Elicitation of missing profile data.} The system assumes a complete user profile. A short elicitation dialogue could ask targeted follow-up questions when a profile is incomplete in a way that materially affects the computed nutrient targets.
