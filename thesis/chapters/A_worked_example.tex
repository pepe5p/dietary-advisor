\chapter{One complete session}
\label{cha:worked-example}

This appendix walks through one run of the pipeline of \Cref{cha:design}, in the order the pipeline itself takes: a lookup, the plan the model emitted from it, the hydrated result, the day's totals against target, the coverage caveats attached to them, and the explanation delivered alongside. It is the \texttt{regular} scenario (\Cref{sec:methodology-scenarios}: fixed macro targets of roughly \qty{2728}{\kcal}, \qty{124.8}{\gram} protein, \qty{386.7}{\gram} carbohydrate and \qty{75.8}{\gram} fat, and no free-form request), planned by \texttt{gemini-3.7-flash} at high effort with the totaller and reflection loop active. It is illustrative, not a measurement; systematic figures belong to \Cref{cha:results}.

\Cref{lst:example-lookup} shows the lookup tool's response to a query for \texttt{rolled oats}, trimmed to one hit per source for typesetting. It returns both channels at once -- a branded Polish Open Food Facts entry and a generic USDA one -- each with per-\qty{100}{\gram} nutrients and the source-prefixed \texttt{code} the agent must copy verbatim.

\lstinputlisting[caption={Lookup response for \texttt{rolled oats}, reformatted for typesetting.},label={lst:example-lookup}]{listings/example_lookup.json}

From hits like these the model composes the day. \Cref{lst:example-plan} shows the breakfast meal as it emitted it: each portion a reference (code, name, grams), with no nutrient values. This single meal mixes both catalogues -- Open Food Facts codes for the Polish branded oats, milk and honey, USDA codes for the generic chia, apple, walnuts and cinnamon.

\lstinputlisting[caption={Breakfast plan as emitted (references only).},label={lst:example-plan}]{listings/example_plan.json}

Hydration then resolves every reference to a food knowledge base row and runs the totaller, filling in the numbers. \Cref{tab:example-breakfast-items} shows the same breakfast meal after that step; the output carries an equivalent breakdown for every meal.

Summed over the day, energy, protein and fat land within about a percent of target and carbohydrate within a few percent (\Cref{tab:example-totals}); the reflection loop reported no issues and ran zero refinement passes. Fibre is reported per nutrient but is not part of the macro error metrics.

Those totals do not stand alone. The totaller also reports which of them are understated because a food leaves a nutrient blank (\Cref{tab:example-caveats}). Salt is flagged across the twenty USDA foods that do not model it, and the Open Food Facts milk, honey and skyr for the micronutrients they leave blank.

Finally, the output carries the plan's \emph{rationale} field (\Cref{fig:example-rationale}): an opening summary, dietary choices, and a supplementation section. The last is the kind of note the agent's prompt was extended to invite (\Cref{sec:impl-prompt-fixes}); here it flags vitamin~D as under-covered by food alone, citing the day's total of about \qty{1.3}{\micro\gram}. That figure is the model's own prose, not a field the system reports as data, and nothing downstream parses it (\Cref{sec:design-no-invented-foods}).
